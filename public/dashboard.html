RaftKV

Five independent server processes below are running a from-scratch implementation of the Raft consensus algorithm — the same protocol behind etcd, Consul, and CockroachDB. They elected a leader among themselves with no coordinator, and every write you make is replicated to a majority of them before it's acknowledged.

⚡ Try it: hit Kill on whichever node is currently the LEADER. Within about a second the remaining nodes will notice, hold an election, and promote a new leader — watch the term number jump and a new gold card light up below. Writes keep succeeding the whole time.

What's actually being demonstrated

Computing gravity or rendering a game is easy to make look impressive. Getting multiple independent computers to agree on a single sequence of events, even while some of them are actively crashing, is the unglamorous problem every real distributed database has to solve — and it's notoriously easy to get subtly wrong. Raft was published specifically because the previous standard algorithm, Paxos, was too hard for most engineers to implement correctly. This page runs the real thing, not a simulation of it.

1

Leader election

Each node runs a randomized timer. If it hears nothing from a leader before that timer fires, it becomes a candidate, votes for itself, and asks every peer for a vote. First to a majority becomes leader for that term.

2

Log replication

Every SET/DELETE you submit becomes a log entry on the leader, which streams it to all followers. Once a majority have stored it, it's "committed" and applied to the key-value map — that's what makes it durable.

3

Fault tolerance

Kill any minority of nodes (here: up to 2 of 5) and the cluster keeps working. Kill the leader specifically and a new election happens automatically — no committed write is ever lost.
